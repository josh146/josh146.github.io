Title: Beef stock
Date: 2026-11-07
Category: Stocks, soups, and sauces
Slug: beef-stock
Time: 3 hours
Servings: About 2L of stock
Sticky: true

A rich beef stock made in the pressure cooker, adapted from my [[chicken stock]]. Unlike the chicken
stock, only the bones are roasted --- the vegetables go into the pressure cooker raw. Like the
chicken stock, I portion this out into 1 and 2 cup servings and freeze for later. Adapted from
[Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/pressure-cooker-beef-stock-2).

## Ingredients

* 1.5 kg beef soup bones
* Olive oil, for drizzling
* 1 (225g) yellow onion (cut into quarters)
* 1 medium (115g) carrot (cut into 2 inch pieces)
* 2 large (85g) celery rib (cut into 2 inch pieces)
* 2 medium cloves garlic
* 2 tbsp tomato paste
* 1 tbsp black peppercorns

## Instructions

1. Preheat oven to 200ºC with rack set in middle position. Lightly coat the beef bones with olive
oil and arrange in an even layer on a rimmed baking sheet or in a roasting pan. Roast until
browned, about 30 minutes.

2. Transfer the bones to a pressure cooker. Pour a thin layer of boiling water into the roasting pan
and scrape up any browned bits. Pour pan juices into pressure cooker.

3. Add the onion, carrot, celery, garlic, tomato paste, and peppercorns to the pressure cooker. Add
enough cold water to bring level to the cooker's max-fill line; do not let water exceed the
max-fill line, even if some solids are not submerged.

4. Close pressure cooker, bring to high pressure, and cook for 2 hours. Allow cooker to depressurize
naturally[ref]By allowing the pressure cooker to depressurize naturally, this ensures that the
stock is not brought up to a rapid boil. This can cause sputtering of hot liquids, but can also
cause emulsification of the stock making it cloudy.[/ref]. Open cooker and strain stock through a
fine-mesh strainer; discard solids.

5. Skim the stock by either carefully ladling off the top layer of fat while stock is still hot, or, refrigerate stock until well chilled, then remove the solid cap of fat that lies
on top.

6. Portion stock into storage containers or zipper-lock bags, then freeze or refrigerate until ready to use.
