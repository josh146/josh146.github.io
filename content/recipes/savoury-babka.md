[[Babka]], while often sweeet, is easily adaptable to a savoury variant with minimal changes. This
version reduces the sugar (while still retaining some, to keep the bread moist and to help preserve
it) while also adding skim milk powder, which helps with rise, caramelization, and tenderness[ref]You
can typically add about 4% of the weight of flour in skim milk powder to enriched dough
recipes.[/ref].

This is an adaptation from my sweet [[babka]] recipe (at half quantities to make 1 loaf rather than
2), with inspiration from Erin Jeanne McDowell's [Pizza Babka](/cookbooks?recipe=Pizza+babka&book=Savory+Baking&tags=hide).

## Ingredients

* 265g all-purpose flour, plus extra for dusting
* 20g sugar
* 15g skim milk powder
* 5g yeast
* 1.5 large eggs
* 60ml water
* 1 teaspoon salt
* 75g unsalted butter (room temp, cut into 2cm cubes)
* Neutral oil for greasing

## Instructions

1. Mix yeast, warm water, eggs, salt, sugar, and skim milk powder in the bowl of a stand mixer.
Using the dough hook, beat on medium-low until mixture is combined.

1. Add flour, and mix on medium speed until dough comes together, 2-3 minutes. Add butter, adding a
few cubes at a time, mixing until incorporated. Continue mixing for about 10 minutes on medium
speed, until dough is completely smooth, elastic, shiny, and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.
During mixing, you will need to scrape down the sides of the bowl.

1. Place dough in a large bowl brushed with oil, cover with plastic wrap, and leave in the fridge
for at least half a day or overnight.

1. Grease two 1kg loaf pans (9x4 inch/23x10 cm) with oil and line the bottom of each pan with waxed
paper. Divide dough in half and keep one half covered in the fridge.

### Shaping

1. Roll out dough on a lightly floured surface and shape into a rectangle measuring 15x11 inches
(38x28 cm). Position dough so that a long side is closest to you. Using an offset spatula, spread
half of the {{filling}} over the rectangle, leaving a 2 cm border all around.

1. Use both hands to roll up the rectangle like a roulade, starting from the long side closest to
you and ending at the other long end. Press to seal the dampened end onto the roulade, then rest
the on its seam.

1. Trim about 2 cm off both ends of the roulade with a serrated knife. Then use the knife to gently
cut the roll in half lengthwise, starting at the top and finishing at the seam, essentially
dividing the log into two long even halves, with the layers of dough and filling visible along the
length of both halves. With the cut sides facing up, gently press together one end of each half,
then lift the right half over the left half. Repeat this process, but this time lifting the left
half over the right, to create a simple two-pronged plait. Gently squeeze together the other ends
so that you are left with the two halves, intertwined, showing the filling on top. Carefully lift
into a loaf pan. Repeat to make the second loaf.

1. Cover the pans loosely with plastic wrap or a wet tea towel and leave to
rise in a warm place for 1 to 1.5 hours.

1. Preheat oven to 190ºC. Remove plastic wrap or tea towels, egg wash the exposed dough and add {{toppings}}[ref]You can
also sprinkle with a hard cheese such as parmesan or pecorino before baking. For a softer cheese
such as gouda, gruyere, or chedder, at it to the top of the loaf halfway through baking.
[/ref], place loaves on middle rack of oven, and bake for about 25-30 minutes, until a skewer
inserted into the center comes out clean with no dough attached.

1. As soon as the loaves come out of the oven, brush melted butter over them. Let loaves cool until they are warm, then remove from pans and let cool
completely before serving. Serve warm or at room temperature.

## Notes

* Babka will stay fresh for 24 hours in an airtight container at room temperature. Don’t place in
  the fridge.

* Babka freezes well for up to 2 months. To thaw, leave on counter or overnight in the fridge.
