Phở, a rice noodle soup dish consisting of a clear, meaty broth (similar to a French consommé),
alongside fresh herbs and spices, originated in Northern Vietnam around the turn of the 20th
century. While its origins are murky (although likely connected to similar beef noodle soup dishes
in neighbouring regions, melding both French and Chinese culinary techniques and spices), it has
since become one of the most well known and ubiquitous Vietnamese dishes outside of Vietnam
(alongside bánh mì).

Here I have a simple, almost one-pot pressure cooker recipe for phở gà (chicken pho), sped up
considerably by using a pressure cooker. This recipe adapts and blends elements from both the
[Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/30-minute-pressure-cooker-pho-ga-recipe) and [New York
Times](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1024023-ph-ga-chicken-pho) recipes for phở gà.

## Ingredients

* 8 chicken drumsticks
* 300g chicken feet
* 2 medium yellow onions (halved, roots removed)
* 2 inches of ginger (sliced)
* Coriander stems
* 1 lemongrass (cut into 2 inch segments)
* 2 tbsp rock or demerara sugar
* 60ml (1/4 cup) fish sauce

### Spices

* 1 cinnamon stick
* 1 tsp fennel seeds
* 1 tsp coriander seeds
* 1 tsp black peppercorns
* 4 star anise
* 4 cloves

### To serve

* 4 servings of dried rice noodles (about 0.75cm wide)
* Coriander leaves
* Mint leaves
* Thai basil leaves
* Beansprouts
* Red chilli (sliced)
* Lime wedges
* Hoisin sauce
* Sriracha


## Steps

### Broth

1. Add a generous amount of oil to the bottom of the pressure cooker. Set it to sauté mode on high
heat (if using an electric pressure cooker) and add the onion and ginger. Leave for about 5-6
minutes until charred.

1. Add the chicken feet, coriander stems, drumsticks[ref]Aim to have the drumstick near the top, to
make it easy to remove after cooking without disturbing the broth or losing meat.
[/ref], lemongrass, sugar, fish sauce[ref]If I have some on hand, I like to also add some
[[shoyu tare]] for additional umami.[/ref], and spices. Pressure cook on high for 40 minutes.

1. When done, leave the pressure cooker to release naturally --- this will avoid the broth boiling,
and preserve its clarity. This should take about 30-45 minutes. Carefully remove the drumsticks,
and shred the meat with two forks. Strain the broth (removing all solids) and return to the stove
on low heat to keep warm --- season to taste with more fish sauce as necessary[ref]Err on the saltier/more seasoned side, as the rice noodles will temper the saltiness.[/ref].

### To serve

1. Warm the serving bowls by either microwaving them, placing them in an oven that has been
preheated at the lowest temperature, or filling with boiling water from the kettle.

1. Cook the rice noodles in boiling water as per the package directions. Once down, strain and rinse under cold water (this removes excess starch, which may cloud the phở broth).

1. Place the noodles in the warmed bowls, and ladle over the hot broth. Serve with the fresh herbs,
lime, chilli, and sauces.
