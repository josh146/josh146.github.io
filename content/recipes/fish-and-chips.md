Fish and chips in Australia is an institution (especially on the Good Friday public holiday, when most places are closed --- but not the local chippy! Every year, my friends and I would do an annual Good Friday Fish and Chip Shop Crawl to take advantage of the situation).

While superficially similar, each component differs subtly from the standard fish and chips you
would find in the UK. While it makes sense that the Northern Atlantic haddock and cod are swapped
for the local barramundi, flake, red emperor, John Dory, snapper, etc., other changes include:

- Sides such as mushy peas and curry sauce are nonexistent and replaced with potato scallops,
  calamari, dim sims, and other fried accoutrements.

- It is pretty common to have alternate options for how you want the fish cooked, such as grilled or
  crumbed.

- For sauces, the main sauce of choice is tomato sauce (ketchup), or occasionally tartare sauce.

And when it comes to seasoning, lemon wedges are ubiquitous (although white vinegar --- not the
traditional British malt vinegar --- *is* available). But *the* seasoning of choice for Australian
crispy hot chips (which are fried in vegetable oils, not beef tallow) is
[chicken salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasoned_salt#Chicken_salt) --- a deliciously umami salt blend.

The recipe below is adapted from [Brian Lagerstrom's fish and chip recipe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=auLBC82e3Sw), modified for a more Australian-style fish and
chips.

## Ingredients

### Fish

* 375g firm white fish (such as cod, barramundi, haddock, or snapper), patted dry, portioned, and placed in on a
  rimmed baking sheet in the fridge.
* 50g flour
* 75g potato starch (or rice flour)
* 100-150ml cold beer
* 1/2 tsp salt
* 1/2 tsp baking powder

### Fish

1. Heat the oil to 190ºC.

1. In a shallow dish, place about 25g of potato starch. In another small, wide bowl, mix equal
amounts of flour and potato starch alongside the salt and baking powder. While whisking, pour in
the beer, stopping when you get to a thin pancake batter-like consistency[ref]You can add an ice
cube at this stage to ensure the batter remains cold.[/ref].

1. Remove the fish from the fridge, and salt each side. Place the fish in the dry dredge, ensuring
it is covered all over with potato starch. Shake off the excess, and then submerge the fish in the
batter. Allow excess batter to drip off.

1. *Slowly* lower the battered fish into the oil, waving the submerged portion back and forth,
before slowly lowering the fish in and dropping it fully into the oil (away from your body). This
process allows the batter to aerate and set before the fish is fully dropped into the oil, ensuring
that the fish will float (rather than sinking and sticking to the bottom of the pot). Fry the fish
for about 4 minutes, in batches of 2.

1. Drain the fish on a wire rack, and salt it. The fish will remain crispy for about 30 minutes.
